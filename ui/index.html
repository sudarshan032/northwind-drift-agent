All data is fictional: Northwind Health is an invented health system sized like a large US one.

The problem

Hospitals keep a , a database of every system and what depends on what. Change approvers trust it to answer “what breaks if we patch this?”. But it : new connections get added and never recorded. When the record is wrong, a “low-risk” change can take down systems nobody warned.

How it works

  1. The record

    The CMDB lists systems and the dependencies people .

  2. The evidence

    Eleven (scanners, traffic metrics, the interface engine, backup and access logs) show what actually happens.

  3. The gap

    Both go into one FalkorDB graph on the same components. Wherever the evidence and the record disagree, that is drift.

  4. The agent

    The Change Guardian investigates, decides and acts: it picks graph tools over MCP, shows every step and why, warns owners and flags risky changes, proposes fixes a person approves, and remembers what people confirmed in its own memory graph.

The agent investigates, decides and acts

Ask the Change Guardian to review a change or chase a symptom. It chooses graph tools one at a time through MCP (the same read-only tools any MCP client can use at /mcp), says why at each step, then gives a checked verdict and acts: a work note on the change, a CAB review, alerts to the right owners, a proposed CMDB fix that waits for a person. It remembers what people approved or rejected in its own FalkorDB memory graph and recalls it next time.

The headline example

Why a graph

  • Walking dependencies

    At 3.47 million nodes, finding the 105,713 components that depend on the busiest storage array takes 38 ms inside FalkorDB against 357 ms for a tuned PostgreSQL recursive query, about 9 times faster. End to end from Python, with compiled clients on both sides, it is 56 against 375 ms.

  • Steady under load

    With 100 clients asking at once, FalkorDB's slowest answers stay lower: p95 1,725 ms and p99 1,944 ms against 4,401 and 7,895 ms. PostgreSQL keeps the higher throughput (142 against 87 queries a second) and the lower median. FalkorDB measured without hiredis (slower client).

  • Each tool for its job

    The graph walks, SQL describes: FalkorDB finding the components and PostgreSQL adding their details was fastest end to end for big radii (714 against 944 ms, measured without hiredis (slower client)); for the full question, PostgreSQL alone is faster than FalkorDB alone (1,324 against 908 ms at the largest radius, with hiredis).

Measured on one laptop with identical answers from both systems. Method and every table: bench/RESULTS.md.

About this demo

All systems, people, changes and incidents are fictional. AI explanations come from free models and are saved in advance on the public site, with a small daily allowance of live answers in Chat. Approvals are real and change the graph, but the demo resets every day and whenever someone presses “Reset demo”. Open source: GitHub, README and benchmark results.

            Scale

            What was measured: the same blast-radius questions on FalkorDB and a tuned PostgreSQL, on the same data from 49 thousand to 3.47 million nodes, on one laptop.
